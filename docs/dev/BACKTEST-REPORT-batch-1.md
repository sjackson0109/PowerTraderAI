## Method (declared before any result was looked at)

- **Strategies:** STRAT-001 (TEMA 12/48 crossover, persistence 2, ADX >= 20) and STRAT-002 (Supertrend ATR 10 x 3.0, confirm 1) at their catalogue **default** parameters. Nothing was tuned.
- **Overlays:** none, versus `OVL-ATR` + `OVL-COOLDOWN` at defaults (ATR 14 x 2.5 trailing stop; 3-bar cooldown after an exit, 12 after a loss). One overlay set, chosen in advance.
- **Costs:** 10 bps fee + 5 bps slippage on every fill; 100% of equity per entry; long-only spot.
- **Fills:** signal on a closed bar fills at the next bar's open. A position still open on the last bar is closed at that bar's close, with costs.
- **Split:** the first 70% of bars (by time) are in-sample (IS), the last 30% out-of-sample (OOS), reported separately. The OOS run starts flat at the split; earlier bars are indicator history only.
- **Benchmark:** buy-and-hold over the identical window: one buy at the first tradable open, one sell at the last close, same fees and slippage.
- Sharpe/Sortino are annualised per-bar, risk-free rate 0 (crypto trades 24/7). `n/a` = undefined (e.g. no trades). Fees are in USD on a $10,000 start.

- Out-of-sample strategy returns range from -65.0% to -17.3%; 16 of 16 runs lost money.
- Ahead of buy-and-hold: 8 of 16 runs (8 of 8 at 4h, 0 of 8 at 1h). Time in market for those 8: 21%–47%.
- In-sample: buy-and-hold returned +268% to +608%; the best strategy run returned +110%.
- Overlays (OVL-ATR + OVL-COOLDOWN) improved the total return in 6 of 8 out-of-sample comparisons but in only 1 of 8 in-sample ones.
- 1h out-of-sample fees were $1,429–$2,185 on a $10,000 start (buy-and-hold paid about $17).

## Plain-English answer

**Not in any way that shows an edge.** On paper 8 of the 16 out-of-sample runs finished ahead of buy-and-hold after costs, but every one of those was a 4-hour run in a market that fell by roughly 30–40% over the window; they "won" by losing less (17–36% down against 29–40% down) while sitting in cash for most of the time, and **no strategy run made money out of sample**. Every one of the eight 1-hour runs finished behind buy-and-hold, and costs were heavy there (110–187 trades each, $1.4k–$2.2k of fees on a $10k account, against about $17 for buy-and-hold). In-sample, which was a strong bull market, buy-and-hold beat every strategy run by a wide margin. The overlays reduced losses out of sample (more often than not) but cost return in-sample, which is what lower exposure does in a falling versus a rising market, not evidence that they add skill. One out-of-sample window of about 13 months is a single market regime, no significance test was run, and the defaults were not tuned, so the honest summary is: nothing here beat buy-and-hold in a way that should be trusted, and nothing here justifies trading real money.
